Any BVAR composition that is not conjugate runs on the MCMC engine: a Gibbs sampler written by hand in JAX. This guide fits stochastic volatility models, sets the sampler budget and reads the convergence diagnostics that decide whether a fit counts in a comparison.

## Fit an SV model

Choose the volatility component; the rest of the model stays as it was.

```python
import rai_bvar as rb

data = rb.datasets.load("fred-qd", vintage="2026-09", variables="small")

model = rb.BVAR(vol=rb.SV(outliers=True), lags=4)
model.engine   # "mcmc"
model.name     # "BVAR(Minnesota(structural, eb), SV(outliers), p=4)"

fit = model.fit(data)
fit.converged      # the R-hat / ESS gate
fit.diagnostics    # one row per monitored quantity
fc = fit.forecast(horizon=8, draws=2000, seed=0)
```

With an SV volatility, `Minnesota()` resolves to its structural form: the prior sits on the coefficients of the recursive structural form, with the prior mean chosen so the reduced-form prior mean is the familiar Minnesota one. Its prior covariances differ from the conjugate prior's, so a structural and a conjugate Minnesota prior are different priors. Use `Minnesota(form="reduced")` for the prior on the reduced-form coefficients; it is sampled with the corrected triangular algorithm of Carriero, Chan, Clark and Marcellino (2022).

## The SV family

Each equation's log variance `h_t` follows `h_t = μ + φ(h_(t−1) − μ) + σ η_t`, in the parametrisation of R's `stochvol`.

| Component | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `SV()` | AR(1) log volatility per equation |
| `SV(rw=True)` | Random-walk log volatility (`φ = 1`) |
| `SV(outliers=True)` | A mixture outlier component on top of SV (Carriero, Clark, Marcellino and Mertens). By default, one outlier every 4 years a priori |
| `SV(tails="t")` | Student-t errors as a scale mixture, with `ν` estimated on the grid 3 to 40 |
| `SV(outliers=True, tails="t")` | Both |

The defaults follow `stochvol`: `(φ + 1)/2 ~ Beta(20, 1.5)` and `σ² ~ 1·χ²₁` (`0.1·χ²₁` with `rw=True`). By default the prior on `μ` is centred, in each estimation window, on the scale of that window's data (`mu_prior="data"`). See [API reference](/api) for every argument.

## Sampler budget

`MCMCSettings` sets the chains and draws. Its `draws` defaults to the composition's budget, fixed when the model is built and stored in its spec:

| Volatility | Default kept draws per chain |
| --- | --- |
| `SV(...)` and `CommonSV` | 10,000 |
| `Constant` | 2,000 |

Both run 4 chains after 1,000 burn-in sweeps. An SV fit with fewer than 10,000 kept draws per chain warns, because the SV parameters mix slowly. Raise the budget for a comparison:

```python
mcmc = rb.MCMCSettings(draws=20_000)          # chains=4, burn=1000, thin=1, seed=0
model = rb.BVAR(vol=rb.SV(outliers=True), lags=4, mcmc=mcmc)
```

`fit(seed=...)` overrides `MCMCSettings.seed`. The same seed gives bitwise-identical draws on the same platform, versions, chain count and device count.

### Timing

The pre-release benchmark fits `Linear × SV` at `n = 20`, `p = 4`, `T = 300` with 4 chains of 10,000 kept draws. It took 52.3 s end to end with a cold compilation cache on one device of an Apple M5 Max. JAX compiles the sampler on the first fit of each composition; `rb.enable_compilation_cache(path)` keeps compiled programs across processes (see [Installation](/installation)).

## Read the convergence gate

`fit.converged` is `True` when every **primary** monitored quantity has rank-normalised split R-hat below 1.01 and bulk ESS above 400, from at least two chains. The primary quantities are:

- the log-likelihood;
- per equation, the own first-lag reduced-form coefficient;
- per equation, `log λ` (constant volatility) or `μ, φ, σ` and the last log volatility `h_T` (SV; for a random walk, `σ` and `h_T`).

Other monitored quantities, such as the contemporaneous coefficients, are reported but do not gate. Change the thresholds with `MCMCSettings(rhat_max=..., ess_min=...)`.

`fit.diagnostics` is a `DataFrame` indexed by quantity with the columns `field`, `variable`, `primary`, `mean`, `sd`, `mcse_mean`, `q5`, `q50`, `q95`, `mcse_q5`, `mcse_q50`, `mcse_q95`, `rhat`, `ess_bulk`, `ess_tail` and `escape`. `fit.summary()` returns the primary rows, with the prior form, the hyperparameters in use, the implied reduced-form prior moments and the ordering in its `attrs`.

A fit that misses the gate warns with the worst quantities, for example `sigma[GDPC1] R-hat 1.202, ESS 9`, and suggests more draws, burn-in or thinning.

<Warning>
  The default budget does not guarantee the gate. On the FRED-QD 2026-09 vintage, the full-sample `SV(outliers=True)` fit at 4 × 10,000 misses it on one of 16 primary quantities (σ of GDP, R-hat 1.012). In a rolling backtest the SV arm converged at 10 of 11 origins at 4 × 10,000 and at all 11 at 4 × 20,000.
</Warning>

The R-hat, ESS and MCSE estimators are implemented in the package (`rai_bvar.diagnostics`) and tested against reference values computed with ArviZ. ArviZ is not a dependency.

## Common stochastic volatility

`CommonSV` gives every variance one shared volatility factor: `λ_(i,t) = λ_i e^(h_t)`, with `h_t` a stationary AR(1) with mean 0 (Carriero, Clark and Marcellino 2016; Chan 2020).

```python
screen = rb.BVAR(
    mean=rb.Linear(prior=rb.Minnesota(form="conjugate")),
    vol=rb.CommonSV(),
    lags=4,
)
```

With an explicit `Minnesota(form="conjugate")`, the sampler keeps the conjugate structure and runs at conjugate speed, which makes it a fast SV screen for many variables. With any other prior, including `Minnesota()` with `form="auto"`, each equation keeps its own variance and the model is a volatility-axis alternative to `SV`. `CommonSV` is available on `main` only.

## Impulse responses under SV

Under SV, the impact matrix depends on the date. `fit.irf()` takes a `date` and a `which`:

```python
irf = fit.irf(horizon=20, draws=1000, shock="sd")               # last in-sample period
irf = fit.irf(horizon=20, shock="sd", date="2008Q4")            # needs stored latent paths
irf = fit.irf(horizon=20, shock="unit")                          # unit shocks, date-free
```

A date other than the last in-sample period, or `which="effective"` (the variance including the outlier or Student-t scale), needs the latent paths: fit with `MCMCSettings(store="paths")`. Unit-shock responses do not depend on the date.

## What the MCMC engine does not do yet

These raise at construction with a message that names the alternative:

- `outliers="lenza-primiceri"` on an MCMC composition. Use `SV(outliers=True)` instead.
- `Minnesota(hierarchy="mh")` on an MCMC composition. Use `"eb"` or `"fixed"`. With `"eb"`, the hyperparameters are fixed during sampling at an empirical-Bayes mode computed before it.

## Variable ordering

Models with a triangular factorisation of the covariance depend on the variable ordering, and every result records it. `rb.ordering_sensitivity(model, data, n_perm=10, pinned=[...])` refits under permutations of the ordering, keeping pinned variables first, and measures the spread of forecast scores and quantiles against a Monte Carlo noise floor.

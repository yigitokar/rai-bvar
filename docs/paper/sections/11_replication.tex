\subsection{Role and target}\label{sec:replication-role}\label{sec:replication-target}

The replication is one of the project's Phase~2 exit criteria, and it is qualitative: the target
is the ranking reported by \citet{clark2015macroeconomic}, that VARs with stochastic volatility
beat homoskedastic VARs in density forecasts, not their numbers cell by cell. % [R] SPEC.md §10.7 l.552; DESIGN exit-criteria map (WP23)
It exercises the whole stack on real data and illustrates the workflow; it does not test the
econometric claim beyond these data and models.
We use the working paper's numbers \citep[Tables~1--3, VAR block]{clark2012macroeconomic}; the
article's tables were not accessed (\cref{app:cr-data}). % [R] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py CR_FULL l.147, CR_PRE_DLPS l.167 (c823d20); N08 §C1, §C5
The working paper states no recursive ordering, so we run all $24$ orderings, with the
table order GDP, INFL, UR, TBILL as the identity ordering. % [R] N08 §C2, §D item 2 (the JAE text was not read; TODO 22)

\subsection{Data and models}\label{sec:replication-data}\label{sec:replication-models}

The data are FRED-QD and FRED-MD, vintage 2026-09 \citep{mccracken2021fredqd,mccracken2016fredmd},
checked against their SHA-256 checksums; estimation starts in 1965Q1, as in
\citet{clark2011realtime}, not in Clark and Ravazzolo's 1955Q1, which FRED-QD (from 1959) cannot
provide (\cref{app:cr-deviations}). % [R] N08 §C2 (1965Q1 precedent; CR start 1955Q1; 1960Q2 the feasible alternative)
The forecasts are pseudo real time on the
final vintage, not on real-time vintages \citep{croushore2001realtime}. \cref{tab:TabF2} lists the
series and transformations, and \cref{app:cr} every deviation from the original design. % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py load_data l.236, DEVIATIONS l.1380
Run-time assertions (\cref{tab:TabF2}) check the quarterly averaging of the monthly series and,
for the primary arm, the absence of look-ahead: at 1985Q1, 2007Q4 and 2011Q2 the resolved prior,
its hyperparameters and the padded data are bitwise identical when the vintage itself is cut at the
origin (\acc{data_checks.in_window.identical}).
% [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py assert_quarterly_averages l.269, assert_in_window l.296, called in main() l.1496 with mods[PRIMARY_SV]

Every arm shares the mean block, Clark and Ravazzolo's Minnesota prior on the reduced form with
$p = 4$ (\cref{tab:TabF3}), sampled with the corrected triangular algorithm
\citep{carriero2022corrigendum}. % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py minnesota l.325
The volatility arms (\cref{tab:TabF3}) are: % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py models l.330, structural_models l.345
the order-invariant baseline \code{Constant('iw_equivalent')} \citep{chan2022asymmetric} in place
of the normal-diffuse VAR; the primary arm SV(rw), Clark and Ravazzolo's VAR-SV; the library's AR(1)
default SV; their variants SV(rw,t10) and AR-SV(0.9); and a structural Mode~I pair. Apart from the
variants' priors on $\nu$ and $\phi$, the SV priors are the library defaults
(\cref{sec:framework:vol}), not Clark and Ravazzolo's, and no prior was tuned. Against this
baseline the SV arms also differ in the prior of the contemporaneous coefficients
(\cref{sec:eval-verdict}). % [V] DEVIATIONS item 5; docstring l.66-67; src/rai_bvar/priors/structural.py::contemp_arrays (SV: scaling "none", Mode I; iw_equivalent: scaling "lambda")

\subsection{Evaluation design}\label{sec:replication-design}

Forecasts come from an expanding window at $106$ origins 1985Q1--2011Q2, of which the $92$ up
to 2007Q4 form the pre-crisis sample, at horizons of $1, 2, 4, 8$ quarters. Both samples are
defined by origin, so every horizon uses all of their origins: the pre-crisis targets at $h = 8$
run to 2009Q4, and the full-sample ones to 2013Q2. Each origin is a separate MCMC fit with its
own seed. % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py FULL, PRE_CRISIS_END, HORIZONS l.141-143; relative_table l.479 (sample = origins; no target filter); 2007Q4 + 8 = 2009Q4, 2011Q2 + 8 = 2013Q2. Whether CR define their samples the same way: TODO 22 (stat minor 12, software major 6)
Under the expanding window the Diebold--Mariano (DM/HLN) $p$-values are descriptive, so a
``better'' verdict must come through the model confidence set (MCS; \cref{sec:eval-tests}), itself a
descriptive route here (\cref{sec:eval-verdict}).

The primary score is the Rao--Blackwellised (RB) log predictive density of
\cref{sec:eval-lpd}: path-conditioned at $h > 1$ in the first full run, path-integrated in the
acceptance run, which also reports the path-conditioned score on the same draws as a secondary
number that never enters a rule; the two coincide at $h = 1$.
% [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py relative_table l.479 (dlps_conditioned, dlpd_conditioned); LPD_INTEGRATED_DEVIATION l.1372
For parity we report Clark and Ravazzolo's Gaussian-approximation score
\citep{adolfson2007forecasting} from the same draws, proper but not strictly proper
(\cref{app:cr-deviations}), and flag the cells in which its sign differs from the RB score's; CRPS
and RMSE ratios complete the set. Verdicts are reported with the convergence gate on, as
\code{compare()} returns them, and off.

An origin is \emph{RB-degenerate} at horizon $h$ when the delta-method MCSE of the joint RB score of
either arm is at least $0.9$: the weights then sit on about one draw, and the MCSE saturates near
one nat however far off the estimate is. % [R] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py RB_CEILING docstring l.355-359
\todo{TODO 14: cite the MCSE weight bound of \cref{app:mcse-bound} here only after it is
derived and unit-tested.}
The flag is descriptive: flagged origins are kept, because dropping exactly the tail origins would
bias the comparison. The ordering stage runs \code{ordering_sensitivity(mode='backtest',
design='all')} with one noise replicate for SV(rw) and \code{Constant}, then a \code{compare()}
verdict per ordering, each ordering with its own backtest seed. % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py ordering_stage l.840; src/rai_bvar/structural/ordering.py docstring l.1137-1151
\code{Constant('iw_equivalent')} is order invariant, so its spread across orderings is Monte Carlo
noise, which makes it a negative control.

\paragraph{Criteria.}\label{sec:replication-criteria}
The replication plan states six criteria in words (\cref{tab:cr-criteria}), and the script turns
them into rules of the project's own making, quoted and discussed in \cref{app:cr-criteria};
criterion~4's rule, for example, compares $h = 8$ with $h = 1$ only. No rule was changed after the
first run. Only criterion~6 uses the convergence gate, and only $h = 1$ scores, which do not depend
on the estimator. % [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py criteria l.666-834; N08 §C5

\subsection{The first full run, its diagnosis and the pre-registration}\label{sec:replication-first-run}\label{sec:replication-prereg}

The numbers of the first full run are history, not results. It used $4 \times 5000$ kept draws
(burn-in $1000$), $1000$ forecast draws and the path-conditioned LPD, after an attempt that aborted
on the failure of \cref{sec:robustness-f2} (\cref{tab:TabF1}). It met criteria
\fullrun{1, 2, 3 and 5} and missed \fullrun{4 and 6}.
% [FULL: config; criteria[*].met] benchmarks/reports/clark_ravazzolo.json (551f181, code 4bb978a); [R] issue #3 04:10Z

Criterion~4 failed on one cell, full-sample unemployment at $h = 8$ (\fullrun{$+1.954$} against
\fullrun{$+0.159$} at $h = 1$), driven by three 2007--08 origins with differences of
\fullrun{$+40.4$} to \fullrun{$+41.6$} nats, where the path-conditioned estimator had collapsed
(\cref{sec:eval-tail}). Criterion~6 failed on the convergence gate alone: SV(rw) was ``better'' at

\cref{app:cr-first-run}). % [FULL: criteria[id=4].detail; relative.full['SV(rw)'].variables.UR['8'].{dlps,dlps_top,rb_degenerate} (12 RB-degenerate origins); criteria[id=6].detail; orderings.per_ordering[*].converged_*; warnings[arm]['MCMC not converged'].example (SV(rw) 1 of 13, R-hat 1.015, ESS 618; SV 1 of 21, 1.013, 550; SV(rw,t10) 1 of 13, 1.020, 537; AR-SV(0.9) 1 of 21, 1.012, 743); verdicts.full (gate off, h=1: MCS25 model in, reference out)]; [PREREG] "Why there is a second run"; [R] NOTES 8b (review of the quick run)

\paragraph{What was registered.}
The pre-registration (\cref{app:prereg}) changes exactly three settings: $4 \times 20{,}000$ kept
draws (burn-in $1000$) for every MCMC arm in every stage, the path-integrated LPD as the primary
score, and $8000$ forecast draws per origin, which cut the thinning error of \code{Constant}'s
estimate at a crisis cell from $0.75$--$1.06$ to $0.09$--$0.19$ nats (standard deviation);
everything else, the coded criteria and the $95\%$ gate included, is unchanged. % [PREREG] "What changes" (items 1-3), "What does not change"; [V] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py CONFIGS l.186-199; [R] NOTES 8b addendum (main repo, b9c66c0)
It was committed and pushed to the hosted repository before the run was launched from a clean
checkout of that commit: GitHub's repository activity log records the creation of the branch at
that commit at 16:10:46~UTC, ten seconds before the run's first log line and about a minute and a
half before its first score (\cref{app:cr-provenance}). % launch: [ACC: versions.git_commit = c823d20, versions.git_dirty = false]; [V] figures/data/provenance/github_activity_phase-2-cr-acceptance.json (gh api repos/yigitokar/rai-bvar/activity?ref=refs/heads/phase-2-cr-acceptance: branch_creation 0000000->c823d20 at 2026-10-23T16:10:46Z, server-side); [R] committed run log benchmarks/reports/clark_ravazzolo_acceptance.log (65dd212): start 16:10:56Z, first arm finished at 88 s, end 19:46:21Z (software referee major 7)
It says that the run is not blind to the first, that the criteria are evaluated as coded whatever
the outcome, that criterion~4 may still fail on unemployment, and that no further run with other
settings is made after the scores are seen, except to fix a bug.

\subsection{Results of the acceptance run}\label{sec:replication-results}

\paragraph{Reading rules.}
The paper's reading rules, which are not part of the pre-registration (\cref{app:cr-results}), let
the RB scores at $h \geq 2$ move with the estimator, the $h = 1$, CRPS, RMSE and Gaussian numbers by
Monte Carlo noise only, and the converged shares and gate-on verdicts with the budget. % [R] OUTLINE §11.9, dossiers/06_empirical.md §9.5; first committed in 33dd9f8 (paper-draft, 2026-10-23 19:25Z), after the run had written its main-stage report (committed run log: start 16:10:56Z, "main stage written" at 1484 s) and before the report was committed (65dd212, 19:47Z), so their timing relative to the numbers cannot be checked
The $h = 1$ log-score gains of every SV arm differ from the first run's by less than the rules' Monte
Carlo yardstick (SV(rw)'s joint gain: \acc{relative.full['SV(rw)'].joint['1'].dlpd} against
\fullrun{$+0.809$}), a descriptive comparison: the yardstick is the largest move over all arms
between one earlier pair of runs, one of which predates a sampler fix (\cref{app:cr-results}).
% [ACC]/[FULL] recomputed from relative[sample][arm] at h = 1 of clark_ravazzolo_acceptance.json and clark_ravazzolo.json (551f181): largest move 0.0031 per marginal cell (full, SV(rw,t10), GDP) and 0.0116 joint (full, AR-SV(0.9)); SV(rw), full sample: 0.0015 and 0.0106; yardstick of rule B (App. F): 0.0044 and 0.0122

\paragraph{Outcome and headline.}
As coded, the outcome is: \acc{criteria[*].met} (\cref{tab:cr-criteria}). % [ACC: criteria[*].met]
Every reduced-form arm converged at \acc{min(converged[*][*])} of origins in both samples, on
the gate's primary quantities, which leave the intercepts out (\cref{sec:limitations}; the report
has no share for the structural pair), and no reduced-form origin is RB-degenerate
(\acc{max(relative[*][*].joint[*].rb_degenerate)} at every horizon), a flag that detects only a
near-total collapse of the weights onto one draw. % [ACC: converged.{full,pre_crisis}[*] (the five reduced-form arms; the report has no converged share for the structural pair, whose joint h = 4 cell has one RB-degenerate origin, 2008Q2: structural.joint['4']); relative.{full,pre_crisis}[*].joint[*].rb_degenerate]
SV(rw)'s gain over \code{Constant} is positive for every variable at $h = 1$ and $2$ in both
samples, as criterion~1 requires, its CRPS and RMSE ratios at $h \leq 2$ are below one, and the
joint $h = 1$ gain is \acc{relative.full['SV(rw)'].joint['1'].dlpd} nats per quarter (descriptive
one-sided DM $p$ \acc{relative.full['SV(rw)'].joint['1'].dm_p_better}; \cref{tab:cr-headline,fig:FigF3}). % [ACC: criteria[id=1..3].met and .detail; relative.full['SV(rw)'].variables[*]['1','2'].{crps_ratio,rmse_ratio}, largest 0.936 and 0.969]

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
On these data and models the run reproduces the ranking that the replication targets (criteria 1--3
and 6; criterion 5, on the variants, also holds). On the primary score it does not reproduce the
horizon pattern for unemployment, whose gain falls from $+0.162$ at $h = 1$ to $-0.707$ at $h = 8$
in Clark and Ravazzolo's working paper (\cref{tab:cr-headline}) and rises here, mostly through three
crisis origins (on their score it falls; above). % [R] benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py CR_FULL l.147 (UR: +0.162, -0.707); [ACC] as above
As registered, we report this as a difference from their results and tuned nothing in response
(\cref{app:prereg}). The pre-registration names three candidate causes: the final vintage instead
of real-time data, Chan's \code{iw_equivalent} baseline instead of the normal-diffuse VAR, and the
library's default SV priors instead of theirs. These are hypotheses, which this run does not test,
although Clark and Ravazzolo's data are in the journal's archive (\cref{app:cr-deviations}); the
pre-registration rules out further runs with other settings. % [PREREG] "Stated in advance" (criterion 4: attribution "with evidence where possible", not tuned; no further run with different settings except to fix a bug); [R] N08 §C1 (JAE data archive: currentdata.csv, realtimeGDP.csv, realtimeGDPpriceindex.csv) (macro referee major 2)
No run-level warning reports a convergence failure, as the first run's did. % [ACC: warnings (kinds per arm; origin_messages: prior means 106 per arm; order dependence 106 per SV arm; nu grid bound 106; sign fold 13-32 per SV arm); no "MCMC not converged" kind, which the first run had]

\subsection{Caveats}\label{sec:replication-caveats}

The illustration is limited by final-vintage instead of real-time data; the working
paper's numbers instead of the article's; Chan's \code{iw_equivalent} baseline instead of the
normal-diffuse VAR, and the library's SV priors instead of Clark and Ravazzolo's (further
differences in \cref{app:cr-deviations}); descriptive significance, with verdicts that come through
the MCS, whose validity in this setting is open; operational thresholds of our own choosing;
a pre-registration that is not blind; one backtest seed in the main stage; and a change of the
primary estimator between the runs at $h > 1$, which is why every LPD number above names its
estimator.

The performance target, BENCH-P2, is one realistic SV workload: structural Linear $\times$ SV with $n = 20$, $p = 4$ and $T = 300$ on a simulated stationary VAR(4) with SV, sampled with four chains of 1000 burn-in and 10,000 kept sweeps, fixed hyperparameters and one CPU device, with a target of 120~s. % [V] benchmarks/phase2_perf.py docstring; DESIGN §10.1
The measured quantity is the end-to-end wall time of \code{BVAR.fit()}, including compilation of the JAX program \citep{bradbury2018jax}, storage and diagnostics; each fit runs in a fresh process, with an empty (cold) or a populated (warm) persistent compilation cache, three times each. % [V] benchmarks/phase2_perf.py docstring

The only reference measurement of the production engine is the committed baseline, taken at commit deb3929 on an Apple M5 Max with desktop applications open, at a one-minute load average of 2.4--6.1 before the six runs. % [R] tests/bench/baseline.json (machine.commit, summary.loadavg_1min); commit d490730
This deviates from the protocol, which asks for an idle machine with at least eight performance cores; the record lists six. % [R] DESIGN §10.1; baseline.json machine.perf_cores = "6"
The median wall time is 52.3~s cold and 49.6~s warm, of which compilation takes 3.46~s cold and 1.00~s when the executables are loaded from the cache (medians over the three cold and the three warm runs). % [R] baseline.json summary.wall_cold_s, wall_warm_s, compile_cold_s, compile_warm_s
One sweep of all four chains takes 3.90~ms, the minimum over three blocks of 1000 sweeps of the compiled burn-in executable, and peak resident memory is 3,043~MiB (medians over all six runs). % [R] baseline.json summary.sweep_ms_min3x1000 (runner rate over the sampling sweeps: 4.07 ms), summary.peak_rss_mb (MiB)
The target is met, but the numbers belong to this machine, load and commit and are not portable.

Mixing is the binding constraint, not speed.
The slowest quantities are the SV scales $\sigma_i$ and persistences $\phi_i$, with minimum bulk ESS per second of 10.2 and 11.2 over the warm-run wall time, against 403 for the own first-lag coefficients. % [R] baseline.json summary.ess (sigma, phi, B_own1: ess_per_s_min)
Every run reports \code{converged = False}.
The runs share a seed and give identical draws, so this is one outcome seen six times; according to the commit message, 4 of the 101 primary quantities miss the R-hat threshold of 1.01 (worst 1.016, a $\sigma_i$), while every group's minimum bulk ESS exceeds 400. % [R] commit d490730 ("same seed, identical draws"; the R-hat values are not stored in the JSON); baseline.json summary.converged, summary.ess.*.ess_min (sigma 504)
Even at five times the default number of draws, this workload thus fails the convergence rule of \cref{sec:eval-verdict}; \cref{sec:limitations} discusses the budget. % [V] src/rai_bvar/settings.py l.76 (MCMCSettings.draws = 2000; BENCH-P2 keeps 10,000)

Several configurations have no production measurement: the stretch target of 60~s on four CPU devices, the reduced-form CCCM sampler, SV with Student-$t$ errors and with outliers plus $t$, and an idle x86 machine (the only x86 record is provisional and was taken under heavy load). % [R] dossier 03 §13.7-§13.8 (x86: git show d37e059:tests/bench/baseline.json)
Timings of design stand-ins and pilot prototypes, which ran different code on another machine, are not quoted. % [R] dossier 03 §13.6

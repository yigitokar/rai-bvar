\paragraph{Scope.}
The package implements Phases~0--2 of its roadmap. The mean axis is linear and the prior axis is
the Minnesota family (conjugate, structural and reduced form, with dummy-observation priors and the
GLP hierarchy); the volatility axis is the one that is populated. % [V] src/rai_bvar/validation/joint.py l.454-480; dossier 01 §2.1
Global-local priors, common stochastic volatility and conditional forecasts (Phase~3), BART means,
generalised impulse responses and approximate leave-future-out cross-validation
\citep[Phase~4;][]{burkner2020approximate}, time-varying parameters, threshold and Markov switching,
factor and order-invariant SV (Phase~5), and identification beyond the recursive scheme (Phase~6)
are roadmap items, not features; for the methods see, for example, \citet{huber2022inference},
\citet{clark2023tail}, \citet{gruber2025forecasting}, \citet{kastner2019sparse},
\citet{chan2024large} and \citet{bertsche2022identification}. % [R] SPEC.md §13; dossier 01 §7
That later components plug in without editing the sweep, runner or forecast modules is a design
intention that no component has yet tested. The data layer rejects missing values inside the
sample, has no mixed-frequency support, and ships no real-time vintages; it only provides the hook
for them. % [R] SPEC.md §1.3, §5.5, §5.7; dossier 01 §7, unsupported item 3

\paragraph{The verdict engine.}
The verdict rule is implemented and unit-tested, but its statistical properties are not
established. The size and power study on simulated data-generating processes that the
specification calls for has not been implemented, so we claim no false-positive rate for
``better''. % [V] no tests/dgp/ directory (dossier 01 §8.2); SPEC.md §10.5
What exists are tests of the Diebold--Mariano size and power, of MCS behaviour and of the verdict
logic. % [V] tests/unit/test_significance.py::test_dm_size_under_null, ::test_dm_power_and_direction, ::test_mcs_eliminates_clearly_worse_models_first, ::test_mcs_keeps_equivalent_models; tests/integration/test_compare.py
The MCSE of a total $\Delta$LPD adds the two models' per-origin MCSEs in quadrature,
although both models' forecasts at an origin draw on random streams derived from the same backtest
seed and origin; whether the factor of three is conservative is unknown. % [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/compare.py _mcse_total l.337-338; backtest.py docstring l.232
Under expanding windows a ``better'' verdict can only come through the MCS, whose validity in that
setting is open \citep[cf.][]{hansen2011model}. Verdicts are per horizon, with no rule
that combines horizons. The Diebold--Mariano long-run variance uses a rectangular kernel with
$h - 1$ autocovariances and falls back to Bartlett weights \citep{newey1987simple} only if that
estimate is not positive, whereas the specification states a Newey--West variance. % [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/significance.py l.35-53; [R] SPEC.md §9.3 l.447; dossier 02 §15 D1

\paragraph{The convergence gate.}
SV fits routinely miss the $\hat R < 1.01$ and bulk ESS $> 400$ targets. The prototype pilot
predicted this, through $\phi$ and $\sigma$, at the default budget of $4 \times 2000$ draws, at
which no SV arm of the quick Clark--Ravazzolo run converged at any origin. It persists at larger
budgets: all six runs of the performance benchmark ($4 \times 10{,}000$ draws) report
\code{converged = False}, and so did many SV(rw) fits of the first full Clark--Ravazzolo run
($4 \times 5000$; \cref{sec:replication-first-run}). % [R] docs/phase2/09_pilot.md item 6, §3.3 (split-R-hat 1.03-1.06, bulk ESS ~200 at draws = 2000); [R] benchmarks/reports/clark_ravazzolo_quick.json converged = 0.0 for every SV arm, both samples (quick run predates f60d736, OUTLINE risk 19: context only); [R] tests/bench/baseline.json summary.converged (3 repeats x cold/warm, settings.draws 10000, commit deb3929); [FULL: orderings.per_ordering[*].converged_sv]
Near the threshold the converged count moves with the seed by several origins
(\cref{sec:replication-prereg}). % [FULL: converged.full['SV(rw)'], orderings.per_ordering[0,8,9].converged_sv]; [PREREG] item 1
The gate is strict by design, and the price is that users need larger budgets than the defaults:
the acceptance run used $4 \times 20{,}000$ draws (\cref{sec:replication-results}). The default \code{MCMCSettings} (2000 draws) are
unchanged. \todo{TODO 33: decide the convergence-gate policy for SV at the default budget.}
The gate also leaves the reduced-form intercepts out: it gates on the log-likelihood, each
equation's own first-lag coefficient and the volatility parameters, and only reports the
intercepts. % [V] src/rai_bvar/engines/mcmc/monitor.py module docstring and monitor_fields (intercept primary=False; loglik, B_own1, sigma, h_T primary for RW SV), at 7f410dc
In the SBC of row~18, the replication's primary arm, about 1--2\% of prior-drawn $T = 50$ datasets
mix very slowly (autocorrelation times of hundreds to thousands of sweeps) in the
reduced-form coefficients, most often an intercept: the row passes at a per-row thinning cap of
$400$ (at the shared $200$ the per-replication ESS rule held for 987 of 997, short of
99\%), and with a $4000$-sweep pilot that rule alone fails (981 of 997), while the Geweke and
$\gamma$ tests stay clean. Whether the replication's fits mix as slowly is unknown. % [R] 7f410dc commit message; DESIGN §9.5 row 18 note and §9.6 erratum (2026-10-23); main-repo NOTES 6; tests/sbc/test_sbc_compositions.py docstring (one run each, Apple M5 Max under load: a Pi element is the pilot-IAT argmax in all 35 replications above 91, an intercept in 19); [V] SBC_OVERRIDES t_max 400; [REL]

\paragraph{Log scores.}
The path-conditioned estimator remains the default at $h > 1$. The path-integrated estimator is
opt-in and covers MCMC forecasts of linear or zero means only; analytic, benchmark and pooled
forecasts raise at $h > 1$. % [V] b9c66c0: tests/unit/test_scores_lpd_method.py, tests/unit/test_lpd_integrated.py; DESIGN D-34
It removes the path-conditioning error but not the Monte Carlo error: it is still an average over
the forecast's parameter and volatility-path draws, whose weights concentrate in the tails. At a
crisis origin, SV(rw)'s $1000$-draw estimate varied across forecast seeds by more than its
delta-method MCSE, and with $80{,}000$ forecast draws the values still differed by about $1.3$ nats
per arm across fit seeds, an MCMC error that more forecast draws do not remove (\cref{app:prereg}). % [PREREG] item 3, "Stated in advance"; [R] NOTES 8b addendum (main repo, b9c66c0: SV(rw) sd 1.4 across forecast seeds vs delta-method MCSE 0.45-0.94; 80,000-draw values differ by ~1.3 nats across fit seeds)
All scores are on the transformed (model) scale. The Monte Carlo noise of the subsampled energy and
variogram scores is not reported, and there is no SBC of $y_{T+h}$ for $h > 1$. % [R] dossier 02 §12.2, §15 D2; dossier 04 §7.2 (SBC predictive quantities are h_{T+1}, y_{T+1} only), §15 Q12

\paragraph{The ordering diagnostic.}
The noise floor of \code{ordering_sensitivity} in the replication rests on one replicate
(\code{noise_reps=1}), and in backtest mode it has no MCSE term. In the first full run it flagged
the order-invariant \code{Constant} arm as material, at two $h > 1$ cells. % [FULL: orderings.results.Constant.material]; src/rai_bvar/structural/ordering.py module docstring l.32
In the acceptance run the flag is \acc{orderings.results.Constant.material}.

\paragraph{Validation evidence.}
Geweke tests and SBC run at $n \leq 3$; the realistic size ($n = 20$, $p = 4$, $T = 300$) is
covered only by the analytic twins, and only for constant-volatility compositions. Our parity
tier has no external VAR-level oracle for structural Mode~I $\times$ SV. % [V] tests/analytic/test_mcmc_twin.py (A3); dossier 04 §9, §10
The replication's primary arm, reduced form $\times$ SV(rw), has been a validation row only since
7f410dc, after the acceptance run (row~18 of the 23 composition rows, one of the 13
MCMC compositions of the contract tests, also fitted through \code{BVAR.fit};
\cref{sec:replication-caveats}); its Student-$t$ variant has neither. % [V] 7f410dc: src/rai_bvar/validation/joint.py _COMPOSITIONS (rows 1-18 and 201-205), tests/mcmc/forecast/mcmc_fits.py COMPOSITIONS (13 entries, "reduced-SV(rw)" added); no reduced x SV(rw, t) entry in either
Test priors can hide bugs: at row~18's test prior $N(0, 1)$ for the initial log-volatility, a
variance used as a precision is invisible, and a G-small run at $N(-1, 4)$ catches it (largest
$|z|$ $13.8$ against $2.33$ unmodified). % [R] 7f410dc commit message; DESIGN §9.5 row 18 note; [V] tests/geweke/test_geweke_compositions.py::test_g_small_rw_h0_prior (RW_H0_PRIOR = N(-1, 4); row 18 per PR, row 14 nightly)
The composition-level G-full, SBC and Tier~B runs and the weekly tier have no committed run record
yet, and row~18's results come from one run each on a loaded machine. % [REL] docs/paper/data/release/{geweke_full,sbc,block}.jsonl. Untracked records exist in the paper worktree, docs/paper/figures/data/validation_record/{geweke,sbc}.jsonl (G-full and SBC rows; the tier G-full row of reduced_sv has passed = false, seed 20261023; its G-full-inexact row passes): when they are committed, this sentence needs a successor that reports them; row 18: 7f410dc commit message (Apple M5 Max under load)
The test-prior design rule $r \leq 5$ is a derivation for the scalar conjugate model, unverified for
$\phi$, $\sigma$, $p$ and $\nu$, and low relative numerical efficiency of some Geweke functions
lowers power. % [R] N06 §12 item 4; [V] dossier 04 §6.6 (min RNE 0.008-0.129)
The power of the suite is measured only against 18 curated mutants. Whether the 10-component mixture
approximation is visible to G-full with unknown SV parameters is undetermined. The reduced-form
parity check against bayesianVARs was replaced by an in-house oracle because the package could not
be built in the test environment, and all third-party parity holds for pinned versions (stochvol
3.2.1, BVAR 1.0.5, BoE bvar 0.3.5). % [R] dossier 04 §15 Q8 (OCSN-10 at G-full); DESIGN Stage 4 amendment (2); tests/parity/SV_PARAMETRIZATION.md "Versions"; dossier 04 §10, §15 Q4

\paragraph{Numerics, performance and platforms.}
The formed-Gram draws that were not rewritten, among them the structural coefficient step and the
CCCM $\Pi$ step, still accept any positive pivot, with no relative floor, although on the old
random-walk path a tiny positive pivot could give a finite but wrong draw with no failure reported
(\cref{sec:robustness-f2,fig:FigE1}); whether reachable states expose them is unknown. % [R] dossier 05 §7 Q1; old-path behaviour at a synthetic state: docs/paper/figures/make_fig_laplacian_pivots.py (computed from committed code; --json docs/paper/data/laplacian_pivots.json)
\todo{TODO 23: decide on a relative pivot floor; commit tests of the old-path failures.}
The excess-pivot recursion removed the crash and the wrong draws of the random-walk volatility
step near $\sigma = 0$, not the slow mixing there. % [R] NOTES 17 (the fix itself is [V]: tests/mcmc/blocks/test_sv_core.py::test_rw_awol_at_a_tiny_sigma)
The performance evidence comes from one Apple M5 Max that was not idle; there is no idle x86
measurement and no multi-device timing of the production engine. % [R] tests/bench/baseline.json (deb3929)
Bitwise reproducibility holds only within one platform and version set: invariance to chunking is
tested on runner toys, and the toy chain-prefix test is bitwise on macOS and agrees only to rounding
on x86 Linux. % [V] tests/mcmc/runner/test_chunking.py::test_chain_prefix_invariance (asserts agreement to rounding); [R] its docstring for bitwise on macOS
The macOS caveats of \cref{sec:robustness-platform} apply.

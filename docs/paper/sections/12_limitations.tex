\paragraph{Scope.}
The package implements Phases~0--2 of its roadmap. The mean axis is linear and the prior axis is
the Minnesota family (conjugate, structural and reduced form, with dummy-observation priors and the
GLP hierarchy); the volatility axis is the one that is populated. % [V] src/rai_bvar/validation/joint.py l.454-480; dossier 01 §2.1
Global-local priors, common stochastic volatility and conditional forecasts (Phase~3), BART means,
generalised impulse responses and approximate leave-future-out cross-validation
\citep[Phase~4;][]{burkner2020approximate}, time-varying parameters, threshold and Markov switching,
factor and order-invariant SV (Phase~5), and identification beyond the recursive scheme (Phase~6)
are roadmap items, not features; for the methods see, for example, \citet{huber2022inference},
\citet{clark2023tail}, \citet{gruber2025forecasting}, \citet{kastner2019sparse},
\citet{chan2024large} and \citet{bertsche2022identification}. % [R] SPEC.md §13; dossier 01 §7
That later components plug in without editing the sweep, runner or forecast modules is a design
intention that no component has yet tested. The data layer rejects missing values inside the
sample, has no mixed-frequency support, and ships no real-time vintages; it only provides the hook
for them. % [R] SPEC.md §1.3, §5.5, §5.7; dossier 01 §7, unsupported item 3

\paragraph{The verdict engine.}
The verdict rule is implemented and unit-tested, but its statistical properties are not
established. The size and power study on simulated data-generating processes that the
specification calls for has not been implemented, so we claim no false-positive rate for
``better''. % [V] no tests/dgp/ directory (dossier 01 §8.2); SPEC.md §10.5
What exists are tests of the Diebold--Mariano size and power, of MCS behaviour and of the verdict
logic. % [V] tests/unit/test_significance.py::test_dm_size_under_null, ::test_dm_power_and_direction, ::test_mcs_eliminates_clearly_worse_models_first, ::test_mcs_keeps_equivalent_models; tests/integration/test_compare.py
The MCSE of a total $\Delta$LPD adds the two models' per-origin MCSEs in quadrature,
although both models' forecasts at an origin draw on random streams derived from the same backtest
seed and origin; whether the factor of three is conservative is unknown. % [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/compare.py _mcse_total l.337-338; backtest.py docstring l.232
Under expanding windows a ``better'' verdict can only come through the MCS, whose validity in that
setting is open \citep[cf.][]{hansen2011model}; with two models it is a two-sided test at 25\%, its
block length does not grow with $h$, and a pair's verdict depends on the other models passed to
\code{compare()} (\cref{sec:eval-verdict,sec:replication-results}). % [V] significance.py l.100-137 (block length round(P^(1/3))), compare.py (MCS over all models; mcs_alpha = 0.25) (stat referee major 2, software major 4)
Verdicts are per horizon, with no rule that combines horizons, and the DM long-run variance
(\cref{sec:eval-tests}) is not the Newey--West variance \citep{newey1987simple} that the
specification states. % [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/significance.py l.35-53; [R] SPEC.md §9.3 l.447; dossier 02 §15 D1

\paragraph{The convergence gate.}
SV fits routinely miss the $\hat R < 1.01$ and bulk ESS $> 400$ targets. The prototype pilot
predicted this, through $\phi$ and $\sigma$, at the earlier default budget of $4 \times 2000$ draws, at
which no SV arm of the quick Clark--Ravazzolo run converged at any origin. It persists at larger
budgets: all six runs of the performance benchmark ($4 \times 10{,}000$ draws) report
\code{converged = False}, and so did many SV(rw) fits of the first full Clark--Ravazzolo run
($4 \times 5000$; \cref{sec:replication-first-run}). % [R] docs/phase2/09_pilot.md item 6, §3.3 (split-R-hat 1.03-1.06, bulk ESS ~200 at draws = 2000); [R] benchmarks/reports/clark_ravazzolo_quick.json converged = 0.0 for every SV arm, both samples (quick run predates f60d736, OUTLINE risk 19: context only); [R] tests/bench/baseline.json summary.converged (3 repeats x cold/warm, settings.draws 10000, commit deb3929); [FULL: orderings.per_ordering[*].converged_sv]
Part of this is noise in the rule itself: $\hat R$ is an estimate, and at a bulk ESS of a few
hundred the rule $\hat R < 1.01$ also fails by chance, often \citep[cf.][]{vehtari2021rank}, so
the gate acts much like a floor on the ESS, and near the threshold the converged count moves with
the seed by several origins (\cref{sec:replication-prereg}). % [FULL: converged.full['SV(rw)'], orderings.per_ordering[0,8,9].converged_sv]; [PREREG] item 1 % [PREREG] item 1 ("consistent with R-hat sampling noise at bulk ESS ~600"); [R] tests/bench/baseline.json (sigma min ESS 504, 4 of 101 R-hat misses, worst 1.016; commit d490730); no committed false-alarm computation, so no rate is quoted (stat referee major 3; TODO list, part 4)
The gate is strict in that sense, and the price is that users need large budgets:
the acceptance run used $4 \times 20{,}000$ draws, at which every reduced-form arm converged at
\acc{min(converged[*][*])} of origins and SV(rw) did so in every ordering, and it took
\acc{seconds} with \acc{config.n_jobs} worker processes (\cref{sec:replication-results}). % [ACC: converged.{full,pre_crisis}[*]; orderings.per_ordering[*].converged_sv; seconds; config.n_jobs]
Release 0.2.0 therefore raises the default for SV compositions to $4 \times 10{,}000$ kept draws
and warns below it; the rule itself is unchanged, and the new default raises the floor without
guaranteeing convergence: the performance benchmark still fails the rule at it
(\cref{sec:performance}), and in the comparison example of the package's README the SV arm
converges at 10 of its 11 origins. % [V] src/rai_bvar/settings.py at 4ac7969 (SV_DEFAULT_DRAWS = 10_000, DEFAULT_DRAWS = 2_000), engines/mcmc/monitor.py::sv_budget_message, tests/unit/test_default_budget.py (CI run 40061724239); [R] DESIGN D-35 (e8cf9f1, PR #7; resolves Q14 at the owner's instruction of 2026-10-24: "4 × 10,000 raises the floor, it is not a convergence guarantee"; converged rule, SPEC §10.6 thresholds and compare()'s 95% rule unchanged); README.md at 4ac7969 l.49 ("the SV arm converges at 10 of 11 origins at 4 × 10,000", Apple M5 Max, 2026-10-24)
The gate also leaves the reduced-form intercepts out: it gates on the log-likelihood, each
equation's own first-lag coefficient and the volatility parameters, only reports the intercepts
and the contemporaneous coefficients, and does not monitor the other lag coefficients, so
``converged'' certifies these primary quantities only. % [V] src/rai_bvar/engines/mcmc/monitor.py module docstring and monitor_fields (intercept primary=False; loglik, B_own1, sigma, h_T primary for RW SV), at 7f410dc
In the SBC of row~18, the replication's primary arm, about 1--2\% of prior-drawn $T = 50$ datasets
mix very slowly in the reduced-form coefficients, most often an intercept
(\cref{sec:validation-coverage}). Whether the replication's fits mix as slowly is unknown; their
secondary diagnostics are in the run's cache, not in its report. % [R] 7f410dc commit message; DESIGN §9.5 row 18 note and §9.6 erratum (2026-10-23); main-repo NOTES 6; tests/sbc/test_sbc_compositions.py docstring (one run each, Apple M5 Max under load: a Pi element is the pilot-IAT argmax in all 35 replications above 91, an intercept in 19); [V] SBC_OVERRIDES t_max 400; [REL] row 18 passes SBC at t_max 400 on the release commit (record/sbc.jsonl at 7f880e1: stage 1, max thin 400, ESS fail 0); the pilot-IAT diagnostics are not in the record

\paragraph{Log scores.}
The path-conditioned estimator remains the default at $h > 1$. The path-integrated estimator is
opt-in and covers MCMC forecasts of linear or zero means only; analytic, benchmark and pooled
forecasts raise at $h > 1$. % [V] b9c66c0: tests/unit/test_scores_lpd_method.py, tests/unit/test_lpd_integrated.py; DESIGN D-34
It removes the path-conditioning error but not the Monte Carlo error: it is still an average over
the forecast's parameter and volatility-path draws, whose weights concentrate in the tails. At a
crisis origin, SV(rw)'s $1000$-draw estimate varied across forecast seeds by more than its
delta-method MCSE, and with $80{,}000$ forecast draws the values still differed by about $1.3$ nats
per arm across fit seeds, an MCMC error that more forecast draws do not remove (\cref{app:prereg}). % [PREREG] item 3, "Stated in advance"; [R] NOTES 8b addendum (main repo, b9c66c0: SV(rw) sd 1.4 across forecast seeds vs delta-method MCSE 0.45-0.94; 80,000-draw values differ by ~1.3 nats across fit seeds)
In the acceptance run the RB-degenerate flag fired only at
\acc{structural.joint['4'].rb_degenerate} origin, \acc{structural.joint['4'].rb_degenerate_origins}
at $h = 4$ in the structural pair, whose convergence the report does not record; the flag detects
only a near-total collapse of the weights and bounds the delta-method MCSE, not this error. % [ACC: structural.{joint,variables}[*][*].rb_degenerate (1 at h = 4, 2008Q2; else 0); relative[*][*].{joint,variables}[*].rb_degenerate and variants[*][*] all 0]; RB_CEILING = 0.9 (benchmarks/clark_ravazzolo.py l.355, at c823d20)
All scores are on the transformed (model) scale. The Monte Carlo noise of the subsampled energy and
variogram scores is not reported, and there is no SBC of $y_{T+h}$ for $h > 1$. % [R] dossier 02 §12.2, §15 D2; dossier 04 §7.2 (SBC predictive quantities are h_{T+1}, y_{T+1} only), §15 Q12

\paragraph{The ordering diagnostic.}
The noise floor of \code{ordering_sensitivity} in the replication rests on one replicate
(\code{noise_reps=1}), and in backtest mode it has no MCSE term. In the first full run it flagged
the order-invariant \code{Constant} arm as material, at two $h > 1$ cells. % [FULL: orderings.results.Constant.material]; src/rai_bvar/structural/ordering.py module docstring l.32
In the acceptance run, at the larger budget, it does not (\code{material} =
\acc{orderings.results.Constant.material}); one run without a false flag does not establish the
diagnostic's false-positive rate, and the flags it raises on SV(rw) rest on the same
one-replicate floor: the spread of two runs, under which a signal-to-noise ratio above 3 is
common even under order invariance, so the flags rest mainly on the fixed thresholds. % [V] src/rai_bvar/structural/ordering.py (sigma_mc = max(sd over the fit and its replicate, MCSE); backtest scores carry no MCSE, module docstring l.26-33); config.noise_reps = 1 (stat referee major 7, macro major 6) % [ACC: orderings.results.Constant.material = false; orderings.results['SV(rw)'].material = true]

\paragraph{Validation evidence.}
Geweke tests and SBC run at $n \leq 3$; the realistic size ($n = 20$, $p = 4$, $T = 300$) is
covered only by the analytic twins, and only for constant-volatility compositions. Our parity
tier has no external VAR-level oracle for structural Mode~I $\times$ SV. % [V] tests/analytic/test_mcmc_twin.py (A3); dossier 04 §9, §10
The replication's primary arm, reduced form $\times$ SV(rw), has been a validation row only since
after the acceptance run (row~18; \cref{sec:validation-coverage}); its Student-$t$ variant has no
row and no contract fit. % row 18 registered in 7f410dc % [V] 7f410dc: src/rai_bvar/validation/joint.py _COMPOSITIONS (rows 1-18 and 201-205), tests/mcmc/forecast/mcmc_fits.py COMPOSITIONS (13 entries, "reduced-SV(rw)" added); no reduced x SV(rw, t) entry in either
Test priors can hide bugs: row~18's default test prior hides a prior variance used as a
precision, which a variant G-small test catches (\cref{sec:validation-coverage}; 13.8 against 2.33). % [R] 7f410dc commit message; DESIGN §9.5 row 18 note; [V] tests/geweke/test_geweke_compositions.py::test_g_small_rw_h0_prior (RW_H0_PRIOR = N(-1, 4); row 18 per PR, row 14 nightly)
The release validation record of the weekly tier is one run on one machine (an Apple M5 Max), the
per-PR tier on the release commit has pass or fail outcomes only (continuous integration on
Linux), so the G-small statistics of \cref{tab:TabD1}, row~18's among them, come from earlier
commits; the x86-64 mutation runs predate the release, and the one x86-64 run of the weekly tier,
also earlier, stopped half-way at its time limit with the replay test of F1 failing
(\cref{app:validation}; the test was replaced after the release, \cref{sec:robustness-f1}). % [V] PR #11, merge 3644c4b % [REL] benchmarks/reports/validation/7f880e1_apple-m5-max/ (README.md, meta.json: one run, 2026-10-24 04:17-07:18 UTC; markers "(slow or network) and not bench"; tests/unit excluded, issue #3); record/geweke.jsonl has no G-small tier; CI run 40061724239 (fast suite at 7f880e1, pass or fail only); TabD1 statistics: dossier 04 §6.6 (62d15f6) and 7f410dc (row 18); x86 mutation runs: mutants.py EVIDENCE header (b27c7bb, 00524a9); x86 weekly run: CI run 40052069815 at 779957f (docs/paper/figures/data/provenance/ci_run_40052069815_weekly_x86.txt) (macro referee major 1, stat referee major 5)
The test-prior design rule $r \leq 5$ is a derivation for the scalar conjugate model, unverified for
$\phi$, $\sigma$, $p$ and $\nu$, and low relative numerical efficiency of some Geweke functions
lowers power. % [R] N06 §12 item 4; [V] dossier 04 §6.6 (min RNE 0.008-0.129)
The suite is checked only against 18 curated mutants, which says little about its power against
unknown bugs. Whether the 10-component mixture
approximation is visible to G-full with unknown SV parameters is undetermined. The reduced-form
parity check against bayesianVARs was replaced by an in-house oracle because the package could not
be built in the test environment, and all third-party parity holds for pinned versions (stochvol
3.2.1, BVAR 1.0.5, BoE bvar 0.3.5). % [R] dossier 04 §15 Q8 (OCSN-10 at G-full); DESIGN Stage 4 amendment (2); tests/parity/SV_PARAMETRIZATION.md "Versions"; dossier 04 §10, §15 Q4

\paragraph{Numerics, performance and platforms.}
The formed-Gram draws that were not rewritten, among them the structural coefficient step and the
CCCM $\Pi$ step, still accept any positive pivot, with no relative floor, although on the old
random-walk path a tiny positive pivot could give a finite but wrong draw with no failure reported
(\cref{sec:robustness-f2,fig:FigE1}); whether reachable states expose them is unknown. % [R] dossier 05 §7 Q1; old-path behaviour at a synthetic state: docs/paper/figures/make_fig_laplacian_pivots.py (computed from committed code; --json docs/paper/data/laplacian_pivots.json)

The excess-pivot recursion removed the crash and the wrong draws of the random-walk volatility
step near $\sigma = 0$, not the slow mixing there. % [R] NOTES 17 (the fix itself is [V]: tests/mcmc/blocks/test_sv_core.py::test_rw_awol_at_a_tiny_sigma)
The performance evidence comes from one Apple M5 Max that was not idle and one 2-vCPU x86-64
continuous-integration runner; there is no idle x86 measurement and no multi-device timing of the
production engine. % [R] tests/bench/baseline.json (deb3929); benchmarks/reports/bench_p2_x86_github_779957f.json (AMD EPYC 7763, logical_cpus 2, commit 779957f)
Bitwise reproducibility holds only within one platform and version set: invariance to chunking is
tested on runner toys, and the toy chain-prefix test is bitwise on macOS and agrees only to rounding
on x86 Linux. % [V] tests/mcmc/runner/test_chunking.py::test_chain_prefix_invariance (asserts agreement to rounding); [R] its docstring for bitwise on macOS

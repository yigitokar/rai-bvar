\section{Forecasts, the predictive contract and the verdict engine}\label{sec:evaluation}\label{sec:forecasts}

Both engines return a \code{ForecastResult}, and everything downstream of a fit (scores,
backtests, tests and verdicts) reads only that object.
None of the scores, tests or estimators below is new; the package adds one contract that makes
them available, with their Monte Carlo error, to every composition of \cref{sec:framework}.

\subsection{Joint path simulation}\label{sec:eval-paths}

For each selected posterior draw $s = 1, \dots, S$ and forecast origin $T$, the MCMC engine first
simulates the future log variances, including future outlier and Student-$t$ scales, which give
the effective variances
$\lambda^{\mathrm{eff}}_{s,T+h}$; it then runs over $h = 1, \dots, H$, building $x_{s,T+h}$ from
the simulated path:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forecast-recursion}
  m_{s,h} = B_s'\, x_{s,T+h},\qquad
  L_{s,h} = A_s^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda^{\mathrm{eff}}_{s,T+h}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad
  y_{s,T+h} = m_{s,h} + L_{s,h}\,\varepsilon_{s,h},
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_{s,h} \sim N(0, I_n)$ and $B_s' = A_s^{-1}\Gamma_s$ in structural form ($B_s = \Pi_s$
in reduced form).
% [V] src/rai_bvar/engines/mcmc/forecast.py module docstring l.12-14 at d58c9ef (structural: A^{-1}Γx; reduced: Π'x; m = B'x)
Because $A_s$ is unit lower triangular, $L_{s,h}$ is the Cholesky factor of the conditional
covariance at no extra cost, and $y_{T+h} \mid \theta_s, y_{s,T+1:T+h-1}, \lambda_{s,T+h} \sim
N(m_{s,h}, L_{s,h}L_{s,h}')$ holds exactly.
The analytic engine uses $L = \operatorname{chol}(\Sigma)$ for its NIW posterior draws
(\cref{app:irf}, which also covers impulse responses and variance decompositions).

\subsection{The predictive contract}\label{sec:eval-contract}

A \code{ForecastResult} wraps an \pkg{xarray} dataset \citep{hoyer2017xarray} with the fields
\code{paths} (joint paths on the transformed scale), \code{paths\_level} (their pathwise inverse,
never applied to quantiles), \code{cond\_mean} ($m_{s,h}$) and \code{cond\_chol} ($L_{s,h}$), and
attributes (\cref{tab:TabC1}).
Its invariants, that $L_{s,h}^{-1}(\code{paths} - \code{cond\_mean})$ is iid $N(0, I)$, that each
draw is one joint path across horizons and variables and that stacking is chain-major, are tested
for every registered benchmark and analytic-engine model and for 13 MCMC compositions, eight
structural and five reduced-form, among them reduced form $\times$ SV(rw), the primary arm of the
replication in \cref{sec:cr}.

\subsection{The Rao--Blackwellised log predictive density}\label{sec:eval-lpd}

The default estimator (SPEC~\S7) is the mixture of the conditional Gaussian densities,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rb-lpd}
  \log\hat p_h(y) = \operatorname{logsumexp}_s \log N\bigl(y;\, m_{s,h},\, L_{s,h}L_{s,h}'\bigr) - \log S ,
\end{equation}
a standard mixture log score \citep{geweke2010comparing,kruger2021predictive}.
A leading block $\{1, \dots, j\}$ of the ordering uses $L_{s,h}[1{:}j, 1{:}j]$, and any other
subset factors the selected rows of $L_{s,h}$ by QR, so marginal scores are exact.
% [V] tests/mcmc/forecast/test_lpd_mcmc.py::test_factored_lpd_equals_cond_chol_lpd, ::test_non_leading_subsets_use_the_qr_factor
With weights $w_s = \exp(\log p_s - \max_r \log p_r)$, the reported Monte Carlo standard error is
the delta-method value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mcse}
  \widehat{\mathrm{se}}\bigl(\log\hat p_h\bigr) = \frac{\operatorname{sd}(w)}{\sqrt S\,\bar w}
  \times \sqrt{S/\widehat{\mathrm{ESS}}_{\mathrm{ac}}(w)} ,
\end{equation}
with the sample standard deviation, where $\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is the autocorrelation
effective sample size of the weights over chains and draws (MCMC forecasts only; the factor is 1
when it is undefined). Distinct from it is the Kish effective number of weights
$\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{K}} = (\sum_s w_s)^2/\sum_s w_s^2$ \citep{kish1965survey}, which measures
how many draws carry the estimate.
% [V] src/rai_bvar/forecast/result.py l.325-338 at d58c9ef (w.std(ddof=1)); tests/mcmc/forecast/test_lpd_mcmc.py::test_ess_adjusted_mcse, ::test_mcse_falls_back_to_iid_without_an_ess

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$h > 1$}{h > 1} tail problem and the path-integrated estimator}\label{sec:eval-tail}

\paragraph{Failure.}
For $h > 1$ the moments in~\eqref{eq:rb-lpd} are conditioned on the simulated path
$y_{s,T+1:T+h-1}$ of draw $s$.
For an outcome far in the tail, only draws whose paths wandered towards it receive weight, in the
limit one draw ($\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{K}} \approx 1$). The mixture $\hat p_h$ is unbiased for
$p_h$, so by Jensen's inequality its logarithm is biased low, and the bias is large exactly when the
weights are heavy-tailed; \eqref{eq:mcse} estimates the spread, not this bias.
In the replication of \cref{sec:cr} (Constant arm, unemployment, $h = 8$, origin 2008Q2), an agent review
of the quick run, recorded in the repository notes, found $-93.6$ with 1000 forecast draws, $-46$
to $-63$ with 8000 and $-36$ with 64{,}000, against $-19.1$ for the path-integrated estimator below
on the same 1000 draws (itself a Monte Carlo estimate), while the MCSE stayed near 1 nat, the
ceiling of the formula: before the MCMC adjustment the delta-method MCSE cannot exceed one nat, and
it reaches one when the weight sits on a single draw (\cref{prop:mcse-bound} in \cref{app:mcse-bound}).

\paragraph{Fix (\code{lpd\_method="integrated"}).}% design decision D-34
Given $\theta_s$ and the drawn scale path $\lambda_{s,T+1:T+h}$, the intermediate outcomes can be
integrated out.
With the simulated shocks $u_{s,k} = y_{s,T+k} - m_{s,k}$ and the moving-average coefficients
$\Phi_{s,j}$ of draw $s$ ($\Phi_{s,0} = I$; \cref{app:mcse-bound}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrated}
\begin{gathered}
  y_{T+h} \mid \theta_s, \lambda_{s,T+1:T+h} \sim N\bigl(\tilde m_{s,h},\, M_{s,h}M_{s,h}'\bigr),\qquad
  \tilde m_{s,h} = m_{s,h} - \sum_{k=1}^{h-1}\Phi_{s,h-k}\,u_{s,k},\\
  M_{s,h} = \bigl[\Phi_{s,h-1}L_{s,1} \mid \Phi_{s,h-2}L_{s,2} \mid \cdots \mid \Phi_{s,0}L_{s,h}\bigr]
  \quad (n \times hn).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The estimator uses these moments in~\eqref{eq:rb-lpd} and~\eqref{eq:mcse} without forming
$M_{s,h}M_{s,h}'$ or drawing new random numbers, so the weights no longer depend on where a draw's
path went; at $h = 1$ the conditioned code runs, bitwise.
% [V] tests/unit/test_lpd_integrated.py::test_horizon_one_is_the_conditioned_estimator_bitwise
With one parameter draw and constant volatility it is exact, matching a companion-form oracle at
$h = 2, \dots, 8$ to $10^{-10}$ (\cref{fig:lpd-estimators} and \cref{app:mcse-bound}).
% [V] tests/unit/test_lpd_integrated.py::test_integrated_lpd_is_exact_for_a_single_parameter_draw (atol 1e-10), ::test_conditioned_estimator_misses_the_tail_that_the_integrated_one_gets
The integrated estimator is the mixture-of-parameters, or Rao--Blackwellised, estimator
\citep[Section~2.1]{kruger2021predictive} with each draw's simulated volatility path in the
conditioning set, as \citet[Section~5.2]{kruger2021predictive} condition on a draw's latent states.
With constant volatility it is the Monte Carlo average over posterior draws of a linear Gaussian
model's exact $h$-step predictive likelihood \citep{warne2017marginalized}, and
\citet[Appendix~D]{hiraki2026dynamic} compute multi-step predictive moments in the same way, from
simulated log-volatility paths and a companion form, for a factor SV-in-mean VAR; we claim the
implementation, not the estimator.

\subsection{Scores, tests and backtests}\label{sec:eval-scores}\label{sec:eval-tests}\label{sec:eval-backtest}

Scores are computed per origin, horizon and model on the transformed scale, with the strictly
proper \citep{gneiting2007strictly} joint or per-variable log score as the primary score (the
others are listed in \cref{app:scores-tests}).
% [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/scores.py l.54-177 at d58c9ef; tests/unit/test_scores_lpd_method.py::test_integrated_layout_and_values
Loss differences are tested with the Diebold--Mariano statistic \citep{diebold1995comparing},
corrected as in \citet{harvey1997testing}, against Student-$t_{P-1}$ quantiles (\cref{eq:dm}); the
long-run variance uses a rectangular kernel with $h - 1$ autocovariances, with Bartlett weights
only if that estimate is not positive. The correction was derived for squared-error-type losses, and
we apply it to log-score losses without a separate argument.
% [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/significance.py l.27-77 at d58c9ef; tests/unit/test_significance.py::test_dm_size_under_null, ::test_dm_hln_uses_student_t. SPEC §9.3 says Newey-West (dossier 02 §15 D1; TODO 18): the appendix text describes the code
Following \citet{giacomini2006tests}, its $p$-values count as valid only with rolling or fixed
windows and are labelled descriptive otherwise, since with expanding windows and nested models the
statistic is not asymptotically standard \citep{clark2001tests}.
% [V] tests/integration/test_compare.py::test_expanding_window_tests_are_descriptive
The second route is the model confidence set \citep{hansen2011model} on the joint log-score
losses.
% [V] src/rai_bvar/evaluation/significance.py l.100-137, compare.py l.152-157 at d58c9ef; tests/unit/test_significance.py::test_mcs_eliminates_clearly_worse_models_first
Under expanding windows a ``better'' verdict can therefore only come through the MCS route
(\cref{sec:eval-verdict}), which results should report.
\code{backtest()} runs the origins from a start to an end date with an \code{Expanding} window
(tests descriptive) or a \code{Rolling} one (tests valid), and at each origin refits the model on
\code{window.select(data.upto(origin))}, so every data-dependent prior quantity is recomputed from
the data available then (R9; seeding and caching in \cref{app:backtest}).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{compare()}}{compare()} and the verdict}\label{sec:eval-verdict}

\code{compare()} backtests every model on identical origins, windows and outcomes and evaluates
every statistic on the origins where all models have a finite joint log score.
For a model $M$ and a reference $B$ at horizon $h$, let $\Delta\mathrm{LPD}$ be the summed
difference of their log scores over those origins, $\mathcal D(M, B)$ the set of declared axes
(mean, prior, volatility, \dots) on which their axis labels differ, $p_{\mathrm{DM}}$ the one-sided
DM--HLN $p$-value and $\mathcal M_{25\%}$ the 75\% model confidence set ($\alpha = 0.25$), the
models whose MCS $p$-value is at least 0.25.
% [V] compare.py l.333-334 (_differing_axes: axis names whose labels differ) and l.259 (mcs >= mcs_alpha) at d58c9ef
The default rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verdict}
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
  \text{better} \iff{} & \bigl|\mathcal D(M, B)\bigr| = 1
  \;\wedge\; \Delta\mathrm{LPD} > 0
  \;\wedge\; |\Delta\mathrm{LPD}| > 3\,\mathrm{MCSE}\\
  & \wedge\; \bigl[(\text{rolling} \wedge p_{\mathrm{DM}} < 0.10)
  \;\vee\; (M \in \mathcal M_{25\%} \wedge B \notin \mathcal M_{25\%})\bigr],\\
  & \text{with } \mathrm{MCSE} = \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_t \bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{M,t}^2
  + \widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{B,t}^2\bigr)\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}\;}
\end{equation}
``worse'' is its mirror image, and anything else is ``inconclusive'' (\cref{fig:verdict-flow} in
\cref{app:scores-tests} draws the rule as a decision flow).
With two models the $T_{\max}$ MCS reduces to a two-sided block-bootstrap test of equal mean loss
at level 0.25, about 12.5\% one-sided once $\Delta\mathrm{LPD} > 0$, looser than the 10\% DM route,
and its circular block length $\operatorname{round}(P^{1/3})$ (5 at $P = 106$) does not grow with
$h$. The set is formed over every model passed to \code{compare()}, so a pair's verdict depends on
the comparison set (\cref{sec:replication-results}). Under expanding windows an MCS-route verdict
is no better established than the DM $p$-value it replaces; both are best read as descriptive.

the origins.

\subsection{Ordering sensitivity}\label{sec:eval-ordering}

Because a composition with a triangular factorisation generally depends on the variable ordering
\citep{arias2023macroeconomic,chan2024large}, \code{rb.ordering\_sensitivity()} re-evaluates a
model under all, Latin-square-balanced or random permutations and flags a metric, such as the range of log scores over orderings, only above its
threshold and with a signal-to-noise ratio above 3 against the noise floor
$\sigma_{\mathrm{MC}} = \max(\text{sd across seeds}, \text{MCSE})$ (\cref{app:scores-tests}).
